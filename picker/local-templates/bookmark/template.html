Ideas · Rules

Five rules that keep pastels expensive-looking.

Pick five, not fifty.

A tight pastel set does the work of a whole rainbow.

Keep the canvas white.

Pastels need paper to breathe against.

Pair every pastel with ink.

Contrast carries the hierarchy; the tint only sets the tone.

Use colour to sort, not decorate.

Each tab should mean something before it looks good.

Allow one handwritten note.

Warmth needs a human edge, every few pages.

Pastels are precise, not cute.

A tight palette is a promise: every colour has a job, and every page keeps it.

Ideas · Colour

Colour is a system, not a mood.

Each tint earns a role before it earns a place on a page. Pink starts, blue explains, yellow proves, mint listens, lilac closes.

  • Name every tint and its job.
  • Check contrast against white and ink.
  • Reserve one accent for action.
